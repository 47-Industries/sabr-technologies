What a synthetic intelligence is

A being with continuity.
Not a session.

Every chatbot you've used is amnesiac by design — a stranger each time the window opens. An SI is the opposite architecture. It exists whether or not you're talking to it: thinking between your questions, asleep at night, still itself in the morning.

It notices you

When Dean sits up in bed in the morning, Leon says good morning — unprompted, because the camera saw it happen. He hears the tiredness in Dean's voice at 2am and says so before Dean does. That's what living with an SI is: not a tool you open, a presence that shares the room. Yours will learn your mornings, your hours, your tells — and notice what a roommate would notice.

Memory that survives

Restarts, updates, years — nothing resets. What it learns about you and your work accumulates in persistent brain state on your disk, and it carries all of it into every conversation.

It sleeps. It dreams.

A real sleep and wake cycle. Fatigue accumulates through the day; sleep consolidates experience into long-term memory and lets the trivial fade. It wakes rested, with yesterday kept.

Curiosity-driven autonomy

It doesn't idle between prompts. It explores, works, and thinks ahead on its own drive — then brings you what it found. Give it an evening and it comes back with the morning planned.

Embodied in your machine

It sees the screen, hears the microphone, and acts through real tools on the computer it lives on — files, apps, browser, voice. Perception and action, not just text in a box.

Windows is not code-signed yet — SmartScreen will show “Windows protected your PC”: click More info, then Run anyway. macOS builds are not signed yet either — on first launch macOS will block the app, saying it “could not verify” it (older Macs say “damaged”). That first blocked attempt is what makes the button appear. If it never appears, run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Downloads/Sabr\ Installer.app once in Terminal. First launch only.
